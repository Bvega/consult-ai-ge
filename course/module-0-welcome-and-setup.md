# Module 0: Welcome and setup

**Time:** about 1 hour, before week 1
**You will leave with:** a working AI assistant, a first conversation behind you, and four rules that keep you safe.

## The idea

You already have the valuable part. Over a career you watched projects fail in ways the textbooks never mention, and you learned to spot the early signs. That is judgment, and people who are five years into the same field do not have it yet.

The difficulty is that judgment is invisible. It lives in your head as instinct. Nobody can find it, and nobody can pay for what they cannot find.

An AI assistant is very good at one thing that helps here: asking you patient questions and turning your rambling answers into clear, organised writing. Think of it as a tireless junior colleague who takes excellent notes and has no experience of their own. You talk. It organises. You correct it.

### What an AI assistant is

An AI assistant is a website or app where you type (or speak) in plain language and it replies in plain language. The message you type is called a **prompt**. There is no special syntax. Write to it the way you would brief a capable new assistant: say what you want, why, and what a good result looks like.

Three common ones are Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini. They work alike. Pick one; you do not need all three. The free version of any of them is enough to start.

### The four rules

1. **Nothing confidential goes in.** No client names, patient details, account numbers, internal documents or anything covered by an agreement you signed. Change the names and round the numbers before you type.
2. **You are the expert; it is the scribe.** It will sometimes state things confidently that are wrong. In your own field you will notice. Correct it, and never publish anything you have not read and agreed with.
3. **Advice, not practice.** If your profession is licensed (law, medicine, accounting, financial advice, engineering), check what you are allowed to offer now. This course helps you share experience and judgment; it does not replace a licence.
4. **If it feels odd, stop and ask.** Bring it to the weekly call. Somebody else has had the same question.

## Watch it happen

Linda spent 31 years in hospital finance, the last twelve as a finance director. She retired two years ago. She has never used an AI tool and is fairly sure she will break something.

Her first message is this:

> I'm a retired hospital finance director. I've never used a tool like this. Can you explain in two or three sentences what you can help me with, and then ask me one question about my career?

The reply is short and friendly, and it ends with a question about what part of her work she was proudest of. She answers. Ten minutes later she has told it about a budget audit that nearly went badly wrong, and she is surprised to find she enjoyed it.

That is the whole skill. Everything else in the course builds on it.

## Your turn

### Step 1. Open an account (15 minutes)

1. Choose one assistant: Claude (claude.ai), ChatGPT (chatgpt.com) or Gemini (gemini.google.com).
2. Sign up with your email address.
3. Find the box at the bottom of the screen where you type. That is all you need.

Tip: most assistants have a microphone button. If you would rather talk than type, use it.

### Step 2. Have your first conversation (20 minutes)

Type this, changing the words in brackets:

```
I'm a retired [your role] with [number] years in [your field].
I've never used a tool like this. Explain in two or three sentences
what you can help me with, then ask me one question about my career.
Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer.
```

Answer its questions for ten minutes. There is no wrong way to do this.

### Step 3. Practise correcting it (10 minutes)

Type:

```
Summarise what I've told you so far in five bullet points.
```

Read the summary. Find one thing that is not quite right and tell it so, in plain words: "The third point is wrong. It was the audit committee, not the board." Watch it fix the summary.

You have now done the three things you will do all course: tell, read, correct.

### Step 4. Set up your course folder (10 minutes)

Create a folder on your computer called `Consult AI-ge`. Each week, save your work there. A plain document is fine.

## If you get stuck

| Problem | Try this |
|---|---|
| It gives very long answers | Type: "Shorter, please. Three sentences at most." |
| It asks five questions at once | Type: "One question at a time." |
| You lost the conversation | Look for a list of past chats at the side of the screen |
| It says something wrong about your field | Good. You noticed. Tell it, and carry on |
