# Module 3: Package it

**Time:** 3–4 hours
**You will leave with:** a one-page PDF of your framework and a simple one-page website.

## The idea

Your framework exists. Now it has to look like something a stranger would trust. That means two things:

1. **A one-pager.** A single-page PDF with your framework on it. This is the thing you send, post and hand out.
2. **A home for it.** One web page with the framework, a few lines about you, and a way to book a conversation.

You do not need a designer, a logo, or a "brand". You need it to be clear, tidy and true. Modern tools make tidy easy. A page builder gives you a ready-made layout; you replace the words.

### What goes on the one-pager

From top to bottom:

1. The **title** of your framework
2. **One line** saying who it is for
3. The **items**, each with its "what I'd do" line
4. **Two lines about you**: role, years, and why you know this
5. **One next step**: "Want to talk through your situation? Book 30 minutes" and a link

Nothing else. Empty space on the page is a feature.

### What goes on the web page

The same five things in the same order, plus a photo of you. A real, recent, friendly photograph does more for trust than any design choice.

### Which tool

| Tool | What it is | Choose it if |
|---|---|---|
| Canva | Design tool with templates; exports PDF and can publish a simple site | You want one tool for both jobs |
| Google Docs | Word processor | You want the simplest possible one-pager |
| Carrd | One-page website builder | You want a clean web page quickly |
| Google Sites | Free website builder | You already use a Google account |

Any of these works. Pick the one that looks least intimidating and stay with it.

## Watch it happen

Linda asks her assistant to draft the text for a one-pager. It produces a title, a subtitle, her three signs, and a short biography that calls her "a visionary healthcare finance leader".

She laughs and types:

> Take out "visionary". Just say what I did: 31 years in hospital finance, 12 as director, led four external audits.

She opens Canva, searches the templates for "checklist", picks a plain one with a lot of white space, and pastes in her text. She changes the colours to navy and one accent colour, downloads it as a PDF, and sends it to her daughter, who replies: "This is good. I'd read this."

For the web page she uses the same tool's website option: same title, same three signs, her photograph from a family wedding cropped to head and shoulders, and a button that says "Book 30 minutes". For now the button opens an email to her. She will add proper booking in Module 6.

Total time: one afternoon. Total cost: nothing.

## Your turn

### Step 1. Draft the one-pager text (30 minutes)

```
Help me write the text for a one-page PDF of my framework.

The framework: [paste it]
Who it's for: [e.g. analysts in their first three years of hospital finance]
About me: [role, years, two or three concrete facts]

Give me, in this order:
1. A title
2. One line saying who this is for
3. The items, each with a one-line "what I'd do"
4. A two-sentence biography using only the facts I gave you, with no
   adjectives like "visionary" or "seasoned"
5. One closing line inviting the reader to book a 30-minute
   conversation

Keep the whole thing under 250 words.
```

Edit it until every sentence is something you would say.

### Step 2. Build the PDF (60 minutes)

1. Open your chosen tool and search its templates for "checklist" or "one-pager".
2. Pick the plainest one.
3. Replace its text with yours.
4. Use two colours at most and one typeface.
5. Make the text large. If you need reading glasses for it, so will your reader.
6. Download or export as PDF. Name the file after the framework, not "final_v3".

### Step 3. Get one honest opinion (15 minutes)

Send the PDF to one person under 40 and ask two questions:

- "What is this, and who is it for?" If they cannot answer in one sentence, the top of the page is not clear enough.
- "What would you do after reading it?"

### Step 4. Build the web page (90 minutes)

1. In your page builder, choose a one-page template.
2. Add, in order: title, who it is for, the framework, your photograph and two lines about you, and a button.
3. For now, make the button send an email to you. Label it "Book 30 minutes".
4. Publish. You will get a web address. A free address from the tool is fine for now.
5. Open the page on your phone and check that it reads well there. Most people will see it on a phone.

If you get stuck on wording for any section:

```
Here is the text of my one-pager: [paste]. Rewrite it as the text for
a simple one-page website with these sections: headline, who it's
for, the framework, about me, and a call to action. Keep my wording
wherever possible.
```

### Step 5. Save everything

Record your web address and where the PDF is saved on the [packaging worksheet](worksheets/3-one-pager-outline.md).

## Done when

- [ ] A one-page PDF of your framework exists and one person under 40 has read it
- [ ] A published web page exists, and you have opened it on a phone
- [ ] Both include a clear next step
- [ ] Nothing on either describes you with an adjective you would not use about yourself

## Bring to the call

Your web address. We will open each other's pages and say what we understood in ten seconds.

## If you get stuck

| Problem | Try this |
|---|---|
| The template has too many boxes | Delete boxes until only your five things remain |
| It looks amateur | Fewer colours, more empty space, larger text |
| You have no decent photograph | Stand near a window, have someone take a head-and-shoulders photo on a phone |
| The tool is confusing | Bring your screen to the call. This is what the calls are for |
