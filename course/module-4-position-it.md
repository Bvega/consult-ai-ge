# Module 4: Position it

**Time:** 2–3 hours
**You will leave with:** a one-sentence offer, and a public profile that says it.

## The idea

"Retired finance director available for consulting" describes you. Nobody is searching for you. They are searching for a way out of their problem.

Positioning means describing what you offer from the buyer's side of the table. The person you want to reach is typically 25 to 40, good with tools, quick, and privately worried about a situation they have never been through. They do not want to "hire a retiree". They want **the judgment layer they do not have yet**.

### The one-sentence offer

Fill in this sentence:

> I help **[specific person]** who are facing **[specific situation]** to **[specific result]**, drawing on **[your proof]**.

Linda's:

> I help hospital finance analysts who are facing their first external audit to spot problems before the auditors do, drawing on 31 years in hospital finance and four audits led as director.

Three rules:

1. **Narrow beats broad.** "Hospital finance analysts facing their first audit" will find you clients. "Healthcare organisations seeking financial excellence" will not. You can widen later.
2. **Their words, not yours.** Use the phrase they would type into a search box or say to a friend.
3. **Proof is facts.** Years, numbers, things you did. No adjectives.

### Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Retired executive offering consulting services" | "I help new plant managers get through their first safety inspection" |
| "Decades of experience across the sector" | "31 years in hospital finance; led four external audits" |
| "Trusted advisor and thought leader" | "I've seen this go wrong three ways. Here are the signs." |
| "Available for engagements" | "Book 30 minutes and bring your actual numbers" |
| "Back in my day" | "Here's what tends to happen next" |

### Being findable

For this audience and this kind of offer, three places matter:

1. **LinkedIn.** It is where younger professionals look people up. Your headline is the most-read line you will ever write.
2. **Your web page** from Module 3, linked from your profile.
3. **Other people.** Former colleagues who now manage younger staff are your best route in. A message from them is worth a hundred posts.

You do not need to post every day or "build an audience". You need the right forty people to see one useful thing.

## Watch it happen

Linda's LinkedIn headline says "Retired". Her summary lists every employer since 1989.

She drafts her one-sentence offer with the assistant, which first suggests "empowering healthcare finance teams to achieve audit excellence". She replies:

> Nobody talks like that. Say it the way a nervous 28-year-old would describe their own problem.

The next version is nearly right, and she tightens it to the sentence above. Her new headline:

**Helping hospital finance analysts get through their first audit · 31 yrs hospital finance, 4 audits led**

She rewrites her summary as four short paragraphs: the problem, her three warning signs, who she is, and a link to her page. Then she writes one post: the first warning sign, the two-paragraph account of the situation behind it, and the line "The other two are on a one-page checklist. Link in the comments."

She sends the post to six former colleagues with a personal note. Two share it with their teams.

## Your turn

### Step 1. Describe your person (20 minutes)

```
I want to describe the person my framework is for. Interview me, one
question at a time, about: their job title and years of experience,
what they are good at, the specific situation they are worried about,
what they have already tried, and the words they would use to
describe the problem to a friend. Then summarise in one short
paragraph.
```

Give this person a first name. Linda's is Marcus. You will write to them for the rest of the course.

### Step 2. Write your one-sentence offer (30 minutes)

```
Using this description of my reader: [paste], and these facts about
me: [role, years, concrete achievements], give me ten versions of this
sentence:

"I help [specific person] who are facing [specific situation] to
[specific result], drawing on [my proof]."

Use the words my reader would use. No words like "empower",
"leverage", "excellence" or "solutions".
```

Choose one. Read it aloud to someone outside your field. If they understand it, it works.

### Step 3. Rewrite your profile (45 minutes)

**Headline:** what you help with, then your proof. Remove the word "retired" from the headline; it describes your status, not your use.

**About section,** four short paragraphs:

1. The situation your reader is in
2. Your framework, in brief
3. Who you are, in facts
4. What to do next, with the link to your page

```
Write a LinkedIn "About" section in four short paragraphs: the
reader's situation, my framework in brief, who I am in plain facts,
and what to do next. Under 180 words. First person. No buzzwords.
My offer: [paste]. My framework: [paste]. My page: [link].
```

**Featured section:** add your one-pager PDF and your page link.

### Step 4. Write one useful post (30 minutes)

One item from your framework, a short account of the situation behind it, and a pointer to the rest. That is all.

```
Write a short LinkedIn post, under 150 words, in my voice. Open with
the first item of my framework stated plainly. Then describe the situation
behind it in three or four sentences. End by saying the full
checklist is on one page and the link is in the comments. No
hashtags, no emoji. Item: [paste]. Situation: [paste].
```

### Step 5. Tell ten people (30 minutes)

Write to ten former colleagues individually. Not a group message.

> Hi [name], I've put thirty years of [topic] into a one-page checklist for people early in their careers. Here it is: [link]. If anyone on your team is facing [situation], I'd be glad if you passed it on.

Record your offer and headline on the [positioning worksheet](worksheets/4-offer-statement.md).

## If you get stuck

| Problem | Try this |
|---|---|
| Narrowing feels like turning work away | It is the reverse. People refer specialists, not generalists |
| You are uncomfortable promoting yourself | You are not. You are handing out something useful |
| You are not on LinkedIn | Create a basic profile this week; we will help on the call |
| Nobody responds to the post | Normal. The ten personal messages matter more |
