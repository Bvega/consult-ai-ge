# Prompt library

Every prompt in the course, in order. Copy one, replace the words in [brackets], and paste it into your AI assistant.

Two habits make every prompt work better:

- If the answer is not what you wanted, say so in plain words and ask again. You do not need to start over.
- Never paste anything confidential. Change names and round figures first.

## Module 0: Welcome and setup

**Step 2. Have your first conversation (20 minutes)**

```
I'm a retired [your role] with [number] years in [your field].
I've never used a tool like this. Explain in two or three sentences
what you can help me with, then ask me one question about my career.
Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer.
```

**Step 3. Practise correcting it (10 minutes)**

```
Summarise what I've told you so far in five bullet points.
```

## Module 1: Mine your experience

**Step 1. Start the interview (45 minutes for the first story)**

```
I want you to interview me about my career so I can capture a
specific story. I'm a retired [role] with [number] years in [field].

Rules for the interview:
- Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer.
- Push for specifics: what I saw, what was said, what I did.
- When I give a general statement, ask for the example behind it.
- Ask at least once: "Looking back, was there an earlier sign?"
- Do not offer advice or opinions about my field.

Start by asking me which of these I'd like to talk about: the worst
week of my career, a time I was right and nobody listened, or a
mistake I watched every new hire make.
```

**Step 2. Have it written up (10 minutes)**

```
Write this story up in four short sections: The situation, The signal,
The decision, The lesson. Use my words where you can. Keep it under
300 words. Then list anything you were unsure about.
```

**Step 3. Correct it and make it safe (15 minutes)**

```
Give this story a short title that names the lesson, not the event.
Offer me five options.
```

## Module 2: From stories to frameworks

**Step 1. Choose your story (15 minutes)**

```
Here are the titles and signals of my five stories: [paste them].
Which one describes a problem that people early in their careers in
[field] most often face and worry about? Give your reasoning, then
ask me which one I'd most enjoy discussing with a younger colleague.
```

**Step 2. Find the pattern (30 minutes)**

```
Here is a story from my career: [paste story].

Find the pattern in it that someone else could reuse. Do not add
general best practices. Use only what is in my story.

Suggest which shape fits best: warning signs, a checklist, questions
to ask, steps, or an either/or guide. Then draft it with between three
and five items. Each item must be something a person can observe or
check.
```

**Step 4. Name it (15 minutes)**

```
Suggest ten titles for this framework. Each should include the number
of items and name the specific situation the reader is in. No puns,
no jargon. Here is the framework: [paste].
```

**Step 5. Pressure-test it (20 minutes)**

```
Act as a sceptical 28-year-old [junior role] who is good with
software but has never been through [the situation]. Read my
framework and tell me: what is unclear, what would you not know how
to act on, and what question would you want to ask me afterwards?
```

## Module 3: Package it

**Step 1. Draft the one-pager text (30 minutes)**

```
Help me write the text for a one-page PDF of my framework.

The framework: [paste it]
Who it's for: [e.g. analysts in their first three years of hospital finance]
About me: [role, years, two or three concrete facts]

Give me, in this order:
1. A title
2. One line saying who this is for
3. The items, each with a one-line "what I'd do"
4. A two-sentence biography using only the facts I gave you, with no
   adjectives like "visionary" or "seasoned"
5. One closing line inviting the reader to book a 30-minute
   conversation

Keep the whole thing under 250 words.
```

**Step 4. Build the web page (90 minutes)**

```
Here is the text of my one-pager: [paste]. Rewrite it as the text for
a simple one-page website with these sections: headline, who it's
for, the framework, about me, and a call to action. Keep my wording
wherever possible.
```

## Module 4: Position it

**Step 1. Describe your person (20 minutes)**

```
I want to describe the person my framework is for. Interview me, one
question at a time, about: their job title and years of experience,
what they are good at, the specific situation they are worried about,
what they have already tried, and the words they would use to
describe the problem to a friend. Then summarise in one short
paragraph.
```

**Step 2. Write your one-sentence offer (30 minutes)**

```
Using this description of my reader: [paste], and these facts about
me: [role, years, concrete achievements], give me ten versions of this
sentence:

"I help [specific person] who are facing [specific situation] to
[specific result], drawing on [my proof]."

Use the words my reader would use. No words like "empower",
"leverage", "excellence" or "solutions".
```

**Step 3. Rewrite your profile (45 minutes)**

```
Write a LinkedIn "About" section in four short paragraphs: the
reader's situation, my framework in brief, who I am in plain facts,
and what to do next. Under 180 words. First person. No buzzwords.
My offer: [paste]. My framework: [paste]. My page: [link].
```

**Step 4. Write one useful post (30 minutes)**

```
Write a short LinkedIn post, under 150 words, in my voice. Open with
the first item of my framework stated plainly. Then tell the story
behind it in three or four sentences. End by saying the full
checklist is on one page and the link is in the comments. No
hashtags, no emoji. Item: [paste]. Story: [paste].
```

## Module 5: Work across generations

**Step 1. Rehearse with the assistant (30 minutes)**

```
Role-play with me. You are [name], a [age]-year-old [job title] who is
skilled with software and data but has never been through [the
situation my framework covers]. You are a little nervous and a little
sceptical of advice from someone who retired before the current tools
existed. I am an experienced advisor meeting you for the first time.

Stay in character. Respond the way that person realistically would.
After about ten exchanges, step out of character and tell me: where I
asked good questions, where I lectured, and one thing I could do
differently.
```

**Step 4. Capture what you learned (20 minutes)**

```
Here are my notes from three conversations with younger
professionals: [paste]. What phrases did they use for their problem
that I should use in my own description? What did they value most?
What should I change about my framework or my one-sentence offer?
```

## Module 6: Your first paid call

**Step 1. Define the session (20 minutes)**

```
Help me describe a single paid advisory session. My one-sentence
offer: [paste]. My framework: [paste]. What people said was most
useful in practice conversations: [paste].

Write: a name for the session, a three-sentence description of what
happens, a list of what the client should bring, and what they
receive afterwards. Plain language. Under 120 words.
```

**Step 4. Write your three emails (30 minutes)**

```
Write three short emails for my advisory session, in a warm and plain
voice:

1. Confirmation: sent on booking. Thanks them, says what to bring,
   asks them to reply with what they most want to walk away with, and
   includes this sentence: "This session shares my experience and
   opinion to help you think through your situation. It is not
   [type] advice, and decisions remain yours."
2. Follow-up: sent within two days, with the written summary
   attached, and an invitation to book again.
3. Check-in: sent three weeks later, asking how it went and whether
   they know anyone else facing the same situation.

Session details: [paste].
```

**Step 5. Plan the call (20 minutes)**

```
Here are my rough notes from an advisory call: [paste, with names
removed]. Turn them into a one-page summary for the client with four
headings: What you described, What I observed, Three things to check,
If the answer is bad. Use plain language and keep my wording.
```
