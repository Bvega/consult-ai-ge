# Email sequences

Three sequences, ready to paste into any email tool. Replace items in `[[double brackets]]`. Send from a person's name, not "The Team".

Every email ends with the same footer: your name, a one-line reminder of why they are receiving it, and an unsubscribe link.

---

### A2. Day 3

**Subject:** The audit that nearly went wrong

Hello [[first name]],

Let me introduce Linda. She is an example I use throughout the course, not a real customer.

Linda spent 31 years in hospital finance. When I ask people like Linda what they know, they tend to give a job title. So the course asks a different question: tell me about a time it nearly went wrong.

Linda's answer is a budget audit that uncovered a seven-figure gap three weeks before year end. An AI assistant kept asking her one thing, "was there an earlier sign?", and she remembered three she had never put into words:

1. The reports from that department were always late, with a good reason every time.
2. The forecast was too smooth. Real spending is lumpy.
3. Questions about numbers got answered with explanations about workload.

That is now a one-page checklist. A hospital analyst half her age, about to face a first audit, would be very glad to find it.

You have situations like that. The course is six weeks of getting them out and putting them to work.

[[Your name]]

### A3. Day 7

**Subject:** "But I'm not a computer person"

Hello [[first name]],

This is the worry I hear most, so let me be direct about it.

The AI tools we use work like a conversation. You type a sentence in plain English. It replies in plain English. If it gets something wrong, you tell it so, the way you would tell a new assistant.

There is nothing to install and no code. If you can write an email, you can do every exercise. And every week there is a live call where you can share your screen and someone will help.

What the course does ask of you is the part no software can do: remembering what you saw, and being honest about what you learned.
