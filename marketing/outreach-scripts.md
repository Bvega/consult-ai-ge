> Linda ran hospital finance for 31 years. Ask her what she knows and she'll say "budgets".
>
> Ask her about the worst week of her career and you get this:
>
> 1. The reports were always late, with a good reason every time.
> 2. The forecast was too smooth. Real spending is lumpy.
> 3. Questions about numbers got answered with stories about workload.
>
> Three warning signs that a department budget is hiding a problem. She'd never written them down until an AI assistant asked her, "Was there an earlier sign?"
>
> (Linda is an example I teach with, not a real person. Your version is real.)
>

### D3. The objection

> "I'm not a computer person."
>
> You don't need to be. Today's AI tools work like a conversation: you type a sentence, it answers in a sentence. When it's wrong, you tell it.
>
> The hard part was never the technology. It's remembering what you saw and saying it plainly. You've got that part.

### D4. The free guide

> Five questions that turn one career story into the start of a checklist:
>
> 1. What was at stake?
> 2. What did you notice first?
> 3. Looking back, was there an even earlier sign?
> 4. What did you do, and what did you decide not to do?
> 5. What would you tell someone facing this tomorrow?
>
> Questions 2 and 3 are the ones that matter. Those are the signs a person five years in would miss.
>
