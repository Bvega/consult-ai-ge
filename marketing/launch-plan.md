# Launch plan

Goal: fill 12 places in the Founding Cohort within six weeks of starting.

Working backwards from the targets in `plan/03-pricing-and-model.md`: 12 buyers at a 10–20% conversion needs a waitlist of 60 to 120 people.

## Who we are reaching

**The learner.** Retired or semi-retired, 20 or more years in a professional field, comfortable with email, uneasy about "AI". Reads properly. Trusts people they know and institutions they belong to. Does not respond to hype.

**The referrer.** Often the faster route. Adult children, former colleagues and managers of younger teams who think "my mother / my old boss should do this".

## Channels, in priority order

### 1. Personal network (weeks 1–2)

The first twelve customers of almost anything come from people who already trust the founder.

- List 50 people: retired former colleagues, mentors, family friends, and anyone who knows such people.
- Write to each one individually using `marketing/outreach-scripts.md`, script A.
- Ask for one thing: "Who do you know who retired in the last five years and misses the work?"

### 2. LinkedIn (weeks 1–6)

Retired professionals keep their profiles, and their former colleagues and children are active there.

- Three posts a week from the founder's profile, from `marketing/outreach-scripts.md`, section D.
- Search for people with "Retired" in their headline in the target fields, and send a personal connection note (script B). Ten a day is plenty.

### 3. Associations and alumni groups (weeks 2–6)

Most professions have a body with a retired-members section, and most universities and large employers have alumni networks. They need content for newsletters and events.

- Identify ten: professional associations in finance, healthcare administration, engineering and law; university alumni offices; employer alumni groups.
- Offer a free 45-minute online talk (script C and `marketing/info-session.md`).
- Offer a short newsletter article as an alternative.

### 4. Community learning (weeks 3–6)

Places where retired people already go to learn:

- Lifelong-learning institutes attached to universities
- Public libraries' adult programmes
- Service clubs such as Rotary
- Volunteer business-mentoring organisations such as SCORE in the United States
- Faith and community groups with professional members

Offer the same free talk. In-person works well here.

### 5. The referrer route (weeks 3–6)

One LinkedIn post and one email aimed at younger people: "Know someone who retired with thirty years of know-how? Send them this." See post D5.

### 6. Paid advertising (not before cohort 2)

Hold until the pilot has produced testimonials and a measured conversion rate. When ready, Facebook reaches this age group best, with the free guide as the offer.

## Decision points

- **End of week 3, waitlist under 30:** the message is not landing. Telephone five people who signed up and five who did not, and ask why.
- **End of week 5, fewer than 6 seats:** extend enrolment by two weeks and add a third info session.
- **Still under 6 seats:** refund, and rework the offer before trying again. See the pilot threshold in `plan/03-pricing-and-model.md`.
